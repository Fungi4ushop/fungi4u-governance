## Possible improvements (not decided, just worth investigating)

- **A THIRD CO2 sensor — the operator's read after the 07-25 zero shift ("can't do with only one CO2 sensor") is right in principle, but the gap is narrower than it looks. Not yet justified; here is the trigger and the buying spec so it isn't re-litigated from scratch.** The room already has two CO2 sensors, and **detection already works for free**: `sensor.co2_shelf_delta` is what caught the drift — the delta stepped from a settled ~+185 to a settled ~+276 and stayed there. **No third sensor was needed to notice.** What co-location bought was *quantification* (the exact offset), and that is the part that costs something — the Inkbird has to leave the bottom shelf for 24–48 h.
  - **So the real problem is not a missing sensor, it is that the Inkbird has TWO conflicting jobs**: continuous bottom-shelf gradient monitoring (its documented "whole value") and cross-check reference for the primary. It cannot do both at once. A third sensor would resolve that conflict — nothing more.
  - **Cheap answer, and the current plan: don't buy. Treat a STEP in `co2_shelf_delta` as the drift alarm** — a settled delta that jumps to a new settled value means one of the two has moved — **and co-locate only when it steps.** Detection continuous and free; quantification occasional and cheap. This is the no-overengineering answer for a one-person operation.
  - **The trigger that WOULD justify buying: CO2 going back to *controlling* something.** Today a wrong CO2 reading misleads a human; it cannot mis-actuate anything, because the fresh-air fan is mains and off-controller. If ESP32 closed-loop CO2 control is ever rebuilt, a silently-drifting authoritative sensor would mis-drive the fan, and redundancy stops being a nicety.
  - **⚠️ BUYING SPEC IF IT EVER HAPPENS — it must be RS485/Modbus, and it must expose a CALIBRATION register.** Two independent requirements, and the first one rules out the obvious cheap answer:
    - **Bus: RS485, not I2C.** *(Corrected 2026-07-26 — a Sensirion SCD4x was suggested earlier in this session because ESPHome's `scd4x` component exposes `automatic_self_calibration: false` and a `perform_forced_calibration` action, which are exactly the two capabilities the SEN0659 lacks. **But the SCD4x is I2C, and that is wrong for this installation.** I2C is a board-level bus — the sensors here hang on ~1100 mm drops at the end of a repurposed Cat5e field run from the panel, in ~90% RH. I2C over that is unreliable, and bus extenders are a bodge on a wet-room sensor. **The whole reason this room is on RS485 is cable length.** Don't re-suggest I2C.)*
    - **Fit: it should be a THIRD ADDRESS ON THE EXISTING BUS.** Power (12 V, Fuse 6) and A/B already run the daisy-chain to the ceiling boxes; the panel transceiver is in place; **Modbus addresses `1` and `2` are taken, so `3` is free.** Adding a device is a tap into the chain plus an address, not new infrastructure — much cheaper than it first appears.
    - **Capability: check the register map for a calibration / ABC-control register BEFORE buying.** This is the direct lesson from the SEN0659, whose defect is not accuracy but that it exposes **no ABC-disable and no calibration command** — it can neither be stopped from re-zeroing itself nor told to re-zero correctly. Many industrial RS485 CO2 transmitters do expose one; DFRobot's does not. **Treat "can I command a calibration?" as a purchase criterion, not a detail to discover afterwards.**
  - **Priority: enabler tier, behind the Spar/demand work.** Standing test — it does not move committable supply or a client this month. **And note the I2C correction strengthens the don't-buy case**: the cheap-looking option turned out not to fit, while the existing `0x006B` correction register plus shelf-delta detection already gives a workable maintenance loop with no new hardware at all.

- **Ledger ↔ Sage reconciliation.** The Supabase stock ledger records sales as operational *units*, while the money is tracked separately in Sage (accounting) — the two are not connected, so there's no automatic reconciliation between units sold and income recorded (see `HANDBOOK.md`'s "Where things live"). Once Phase C real-data capture is running, connecting them — or at minimum a routine to cross-check ledger sales against Sage income — would catch discrepancies. Not urgent while Sage is underused and the ledger has no real data yet; revisit when both carry real numbers.

Claude Code supports hooks that could prompt this automatically instead. Not set up — deliberately not added without being asked, since it's one more piece of configuration to maintain — but worth a look if the manual habit turns out to be unreliable in practice.
