## What's broken or in progress

- **CIRCULATION FAN IS DEAD AND OUT OF THE ROOM — and the fans were the WRONG PART all along (2026-07-17).** Root cause of the low-RH alarm. Both fans are **SEAFLO SFIB1-130-01 marine *bilge blowers*** — 12V, 220 m³/h, and rated by SEAFLO at **600–700 hours of *intermittent* duty (~3h/day)**. They are meant to ventilate a boat's bilge for a few minutes before you start the engine. **This system runs them 24/7 = 720 h/month, so the entire rated life is consumed in about a month.** The failed fan lasted a few months (~3× its rating) — it over-performed, then wore out. Symptoms: needed a manual spin to start then ran slow, bearings free, and **cleaned and still faulty on a direct 12V supply** (so not the MOSFET, wiring or a connector — a worn-out motor, not a dirty one).
  - **How it hid**: it wore out gradually *while running* (a spinning motor keeps spinning — it loses starting torque, not running torque). The **2026-07-16 13:52 reflash stopped it**, and worn out, it could not restart. **The reflash broke nothing; it just stopped the fan once.** HA showed "82%" throughout, because these are **2-wire fans with no tacho — HA displays the *commanded* PWM and cannot tell a spinning fan from a dead one.** RH fell ~90% → ~80%, the humidifier pinned at 100% duty, and `rh_low` fired ~20h late pointing at humidity — a symptom. Full detail: `DECISIONS.md` (2026-07-17) and `stock-control/docs/HARDWARE_REFERENCE.md` ("Fan failure mode: WRONG FAN FOR THE JOB").
  - **Current state**: fan removed, **return duct sealed**, room on the **fresh-air fan alone**, holding **RH ~82%, absolute humidity ~12.35 g/m³** (vs 12.9 baseline, 11.4 during the outage) — above the 80% alarm, below grey oyster's 85–95%. **Sealing the duct alone recovered most of the loss**, because with the dead fan in an *open* return duct the plenum was bleeding humidified air backwards into the grow room. **No mixing** now, so expect stratification — and the RH sensor hangs at ceiling level while the mushrooms sit lower, so **trust it less than usual until a fan is in.**
  - **DO NOT buy another SFIB1-130-01 for a 24/7 position** — it is a ~1–3 month consumable in this application, forever. **Buy a continuous-duty fan** (30,000–70,000h = 3.5–8 years). Identified: **RAM Mixed-Flow Inline 100mm** — 199 m³/h, 23/26W, 220–240V, 2-speed, mixed-flow (high static pressure, which the holed 110mm duct needs), **~R890–970**, widely out of stock (live 2026-07-17 at Happy Harvest R890 / hydroponic.co.za R970; Grow Folk R699 out; Cape Canna worth a call, 084 754 2033). **Don't size up to 150mm just because it's in stock** — 580 m³/h into a duct designed for 220 contradicts the low-velocity design bet.
  - **The two fan positions are no longer interchangeable — buy ONE, not two.** Fan 1 (circulation) is fixed-speed and nothing automates it, so a 2-speed mains fan drops straight into **free multiplug socket 1 or 4** with no firmware change. **Fan 2 (fresh air) runs the proportional CO2 loop (40–85%) and a 2-speed mains fan cannot do that** — it needs a continuous-duty **12V** brushless (keeping the PWM wiring) or an **EC fan with 0–10V input** (ESP32 PWM + RC filter — a project). Unresolved.
  - **⚠️ The fresh-air fan is the bigger risk**: same worn-out model, same 24/7 duty, certainly past 600–700h, **and it is currently the room's only fan** — and it runs CO2 control. Keeping one SEAFLO as an emergency spare for that position is reasonable *only* until the variable-speed replacement is solved.
  - **Queued: RPM feedback** — a 4-pin PWM fan with tacho on **GPIO21 (free)** makes this alarmable instead of invisible. Needs W30–W35 rewired plus a firmware change; fold it into the Fan 2 rework, since that position is being redesigned anyway.
